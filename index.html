SECTION 01
CIRCUIT OVERVIEW

The FAN-K11 is a straightforward analog PWM controller built around the classic NE555 timer configured in astable mode. The entire circuit can be understood as four cooperating networks: power supply, power indication, PWM generation, and fan output. There are no microcontrollers, no firmware, and no digital logic — just passive components and two ICs working together.

HOW IT WORKS

12V enters through either the SATA connector or the barrel jack — both feed the same fused rail. The 78L05 linear regulator steps 12V down to a stable 5V rail which powers the NE555 and the power indicator LED.

The NE555 runs in astable mode, continuously oscillating and generating a square wave on its Pin 3 output. The frequency is set by R1, R2, and C3, while the duty cycle — the ratio of on-time to off-time — is controlled by the potentiometer through a diode steering network (D1, D2).

The PWM signal from Pin 3 drives all six fan headers simultaneously. Since 4-pin PWM fans regulate their own internal motor speed in response to the PWM duty cycle on Pin 4, the board needs only to provide the signal — the fans themselves do the speed control.

SECTION 03
CIRCUIT NETWORKS

The circuit is divided into four functional networks. Each group of components works together to perform a specific job. Understanding what each network does makes it straightforward to identify which network has failed when something goes wrong.

⚡
POWER INPUT
12V SUPPLY · FUSE PROTECTION

Two independent 12V sources — SATA connector (Pin 13) and barrel jack — feed the same fused rail in parallel. Only one source needs to be connected at a time. The two inputs are simply tied together before the fuse, so whichever is plugged in supplies the board.

The fuse (F1, 5A) protects the entire board from overcurrent caused by a downstream short. C1 (0.22µF) sits across the 12V rail at the regulator input, filtering out high-frequency noise before it reaches the 78L05. All fan Pin 1 connections also draw directly from this 12V rail.

SATA BARREL F1 5A C1 0.22µF
⬇
VOLTAGE REGULATION
78L05 · 5V RAIL · DECOUPLING

The 78L05 is a fixed linear regulator that steps 12V down to a stable 5V output regardless of input voltage variations. This 5V rail powers the NE555 (Pin 8, Pin 4) and the LED indicator branch.

C2 (0.33µF) is placed across the 5V output to GND, suppressing oscillation and noise on the output rail — a standard requirement for linear regulators. Without it, the rail can become noisy and cause erratic NE555 behaviour. The 12V fans bypass the regulator entirely and draw from the raw 12V rail.

78L05 C2 0.33µF
◉
POWER INDICATOR
R3 · LED1 · GND

A simple series circuit: 5V Vout feeds R3 (4.7kΩ), which limits current to the LED, whose cathode connects to ground. The LED lights whenever the 5V rail is present, providing a visual confirmation that the regulator is running and the board is powered.

R3 sets LED current to approximately (5V − 2V forward drop) ÷ 4700Ω ≈ 0.64mA — well within safe limits for a standard LED. This network is entirely independent of the PWM oscillator; a fault here affects only the indicator and nothing else.

R3 4.7kΩ LED1
〜
PWM OSCILLATOR
NE555 · TIMING NETWORK · POT · DIODES

The NE555 is configured in astable mode — it oscillates continuously, producing a square wave on Pin 3. The frequency is approximately 1.44 ÷ ((R1 + R2) × C3) ≈ 22kHz, which is within the Intel 4-pin PWM specification range of 21–28kHz.

Duty cycle is controlled by a diode steering network using D1 and D2 to separate charge and discharge paths through the potentiometer. The pot is wired as a rheostat: its left leg and wiper are shorted together and connect to D1's cathode. D2's anode connects to the right leg. As the pot is turned, resistance changes in the timing network, shifting the duty cycle between approximately 10% and 90%.

R1 (1kΩ) connects 5V to Pin 7 and also shares its pad with D1's anode and one pad of R2 — this is the key junction node. C3 (33nF) is the timing capacitor between Pin 2/6 and GND. C4 (10nF) on Pin 5 prevents external noise from shifting the internal voltage comparator thresholds.

NE555 R1 1kΩ R2 1kΩ C3 33nF C4 10nF D1 D2 RV1 90kΩ
💨
FAN OUTPUT
6× 4-PIN PWM HEADERS

All six fan headers are wired identically in parallel: Pin 1 (12V) from the raw fused rail, Pin 2 (GND) to common ground, Pin 3 (tach) left unconnected, and Pin 4 (PWM) driven by NE555 Pin 3.

Because all six headers share the same PWM signal, all fans run at the same speed simultaneously. 4-pin PWM fans contain their own internal speed controller — the board only needs to provide the PWM signal; it does not directly switch fan power. This means the board's output stage draws almost no current itself; the fans draw their 12V power directly from the main rail.

FAN1–FAN6 12V → PIN 1 PWM → PIN 4
SECTION 04
FAULT GUIDE

Because the circuit is divided into distinct networks, failures produce predictable and identifiable symptoms. Use the symptom you observe to narrow down which network is at fault, then check the specific components within it.

SYMPTOM QUICK REFERENCE
SYMPTOM
Board completely dead — no LED, no fans
→ Check: F1 (blown fuse) or C1 (shorted)
SYMPTOM
LED off, fans run at full speed
→ Check: 78L05 dead or C2 shorted (5V rail gone)
SYMPTOM
LED on, fans full speed, pot does nothing
→ Check: C3 open (primary fault on this unit), NE555 dead
SYMPTOM
LED on, fans stopped, pot does nothing
→ Check: C3 shorted, R1 open, or Pin 4 pulled low
SYMPTOM
Pot has reduced range — can't reach min or max
→ Check: D1 or D2 leaky/open — diode test both
SYMPTOM
Fan speed hunts / fluctuates randomly
→ Check: C2 open (noisy 5V rail), or C4 open (noisy CV pin)
SYMPTOM
LED dark, everything else works normally
→ Check: R3 open or LED1 dead — purely cosmetic
